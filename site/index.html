The Singularity Workshop · Flagship Experience

A world you can live in.
A war you can change.

raWWar is a science-fiction living-world Experience. Not a stack of missions. Not an RTS with an FPS bolted on. One shared universe, filled with people, places, organizations, work, conflict, discovery, and consequences.

The machinery exists to make the world matter. The people inside it are the point.

01 · One continuous universe

The world doesn't end at the edge of the battlefield.

Military bases, settlements, stations, ships, research sites, trade routes, remote frontiers, and ancient ruins belong to one connected world. What happens in one place can change the lives and opportunities of people somewhere else.

A supply ship is late. A research team needs a rare component. A damaged station loses a service. A commander changes an order. A civilian crew takes a dangerous contract. These are not isolated minigames: they are different activities touching shared people, assets, places, events, and history.

The Commander and the person loading the launcher are looking at the same authoritative world from different positions.

Distance matters. Information takes time to travel. Ships and organizations need people, supplies, maintenance, and preparation. A decision can outlive the mission in which it was made.

02 · More than one way to belong

You are a person, not a class selector.

Choose how close you want to be to the action, how much responsibility you want to carry, and what kind of life you want to build. These are design directions for one shared universe, not separate game modes with separate worlds.

◈

Serve, lead, support

Train as a soldier, lead a squad, command an operation, maintain equipment, move supplies, provide medical care, or conduct intelligence work.

⌁

Build and discover

Research unfamiliar systems, run a laboratory, construct infrastructure, recover lost technology, or solve the practical problems that keep a place alive.

✦

Travel and trade

Fly freight, crew a passenger ship, explore frontier routes, operate a business, or make a living moving people and material through a dangerous galaxy.

⚖

Shape institutions

Take part in civic life, law, governance, journalism, public services, or the competing interests that determine how communities are run.

⌖

Live independently

Become an explorer, bounty hunter, contractor, salvager, outlaw, or someone who simply wants a quiet life beyond the main routes.

◎

Direct an organization

Own or manage a fleet, company, research group, settlement service, or other enterprise. Delegate the work; remain accountable for its consequences.

War is possible and consequential; it is not a compulsory occupation. The design goal is freedom to create, join, resist, or avoid conflict—not a system that forces every player into a war.

03 · The past is still here

History leaves things behind.

Ancient settlements, abandoned infrastructure, fortresses, trenches, wrecked ships, drifting debris, sealed laboratories, and buried archives are not random decoration. They are places with causes, timelines, materials, damage, and stories people may disagree about.

Explore evidence, not just exposition.

A derelict ship may contain a damaged archive. A battlefield may preserve evidence that contradicts the official account. An abandoned laboratory may still have power, hazards, or records that reveal why it was sealed.

Finding an object is not the same as understanding it. Records can be incomplete, damaged, forged, encrypted, or contradictory. Who knows what happened—and who is allowed to know—matters.

Storybuilder-authored history is intended to give places depth and provenance. Authored facts, generated proposals, physical evidence, disputed accounts, and player knowledge remain distinct. Unknown details stay unknown until established or discovered.

04 · A world made of work

A base is a machine made of people.

Facilities do not operate by existing on a map. They depend on trained people, procedures, power, materials, transport, maintenance, scheduling, and time.

People and readiness

Qualifications, staffing, fatigue, experience, relationships, and responsibility affect what an organization can actually do.

Industry and logistics

Resources must be extracted, processed, manufactured, transported, stored, and maintained. Depletion has consequences.

Research and change

New capabilities need discovery, testing, production, training, deployment, and long-term support—not just an unlock notification.

05 · See a real piece of the work

From concept to inspectable experiment.

The project is being built in slices. A visitor should be able to distinguish a visual concept from an executable experiment and both from a completed playable Experience.

Soldier Field — Event Horizon Scaling Lab

Open the live browser lab to inspect a procedural exoskeleton blockout, change the population workload, and review its bounded CPU/GPU measurements. The same source geometry also generates checked-in preview views.

What it proves: a real procedural mesh and a bounded browser rendering/measurement experiment. What it does not prove: finished character art, a production renderer, a complete raWWar simulation, or an end-to-end playable game.
