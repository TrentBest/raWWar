# raWWar — Political Power, Speeches, and Factional Endgame

**Status:** Living design model.  
**Canon:** Politics is an active form of gameplay. The player can address a faction's political body, build alliances, influence policy, and use collective political power to change the war.  
**Candidate:** Exact political institutions, voting rules, speech mechanics, audience composition, and faction-specific procedures remain to be illuminated.

> **Politics is not a menu. It is standing in front of the people who can change the galaxy.**

## 1. Politics is a physical experience

Political gameplay should remain consistent with raWWar's first-person principle.

The player does not open a diplomacy screen and move abstract influence points.

The player can physically enter political spaces:

- faction assemblies;
- legislative halls;
- military councils;
- industrial councils;
- scientific bodies;
- regional conventions;
- emergency sessions;
- alliance conferences;
- memorials;
- public demonstrations;
- private negotiations.

The largest political gatherings may contain **hundreds of thousands of people** in enormous purpose-built halls.

The scale matters.

The player should be able to look out over a living political body and understand:

> These are the people whose decisions I am trying to change.

The hall is therefore itself an Experience.

## 2. The political body is not a health bar

The audience is composed of people and organizations with interests.

Its response can emerge from:

- faction dispositions;
- current events;
- historical memory;
- recent victories and defeats;
- casualties;
- economic conditions;
- resource availability;
- political relationships;
- the player's reputation;
- the player's record;
- the player's rank and authority;
- promises made previously;
- visible evidence;
- speeches;
- allies;
- opposition;
- propaganda;
- scandals;
- fear;
- hope;
- material self-interest.

A speech should not simply produce:

`PoliticalInfluence += 10`

Instead:

`Intent + Evidence + Reputation + Audience Interests + Political Context → Audience Response`

The response may include:

- support;
- opposition;
- hesitation;
- demands;
- counterarguments;
- demonstrations;
- alliance offers;
- political concessions;
- votes;
- appointments;
- funding;
- military commitments;
- resource commitments;
- diplomatic action.

## 3. Speeches are gameplay

The player can speak before a faction's political body.

The player may:

- explain a military victory;
- request support;
- defend a controversial action;
- propose an alliance;
- expose an enemy;
- argue for war;
- argue for peace;
- request resources;
- defend civilians;
- promise reforms;
- challenge leadership;
- rally people after defeat;
- negotiate a coalition;
- ask allied factions to commit forces;
- present evidence;
- accept responsibility for failure;
- deliberately manipulate the audience.

The speech itself should be a player-controlled interaction.

The player may prepare beforehand by:

- consulting advisors;
- researching the audience;
- reviewing historical grievances;
- collecting evidence;
- identifying allies;
- anticipating objections;
- deciding what to reveal;
- preparing demonstrations;
- selecting witnesses;
- arranging political support.

The better the player understands the audience, the more effectively they can communicate.

## 4. Political influence is earned through reality

The strongest political argument should often be something the player actually accomplished.

A commander who says:

> "Give me the resources to defend this world."

is more persuasive if the player has previously:

- defended worlds successfully;
- protected civilians;
- maintained supply lines;
- kept soldiers equipped;
- delivered promised resources;
- built productive infrastructure;
- honored alliances;
- accepted responsibility for failures.

Likewise, a commander who repeatedly wastes soldiers, breaks promises, or starves an army should not become politically powerful merely because the player selected the correct dialogue option.

> **The player's history is part of the speech.**

## 5. Alliances are political commitments

The player can build alliances through political work.

An alliance can involve:

- mutual defense;
- resource sharing;
- technology exchange;
- intelligence sharing;
- military coordination;
- trade;
- research cooperation;
- construction assistance;
- personnel exchange;
- political recognition;
- territorial agreements;
- succession arrangements;
- coordinated action against the Empire.

An alliance is not merely a permanent +relationship modifier.

It creates obligations.

If the player promises to defend an ally and then fails to do so, the political consequences should propagate through the relationship graph.

Likewise, an alliance can make a faction stronger without making it obedient.

> **An ally is someone whose interests now intersect with yours, not someone you own.**

## 6. Coalition politics

A major political objective can require cooperation among several factions.

The player may attempt to assemble a coalition against:

- the Empress;
- an expansionist faction;
- a military threat;
- an economic monopoly;
- a regional crisis;
- a shared enemy.

Thirteen factions mean coalition formation can be genuinely variable.

Possible outcomes include:

- two-faction agreements;
- temporary regional coalitions;
- three-way military pacts;
- economic blocs;
- research alliances;
- anti-Imperial coalitions;
- coalitions that collapse after victory;
- coalitions whose members immediately begin competing over the spoils.

The player should never be guaranteed a stable grand alliance.

## 7. Political power can change the military world

Politics should reach the physical simulation.

A successful political effort may result in:

- soldiers being assigned;
- fleets being committed;
- factories receiving priority;
- resources being released;
- construction being authorized;
- research being funded;
- intelligence operations being approved;
- alliances opening supply routes;
- borders being opened;
- embargoes being imposed;
- military objectives changing;
- commanders being appointed or removed.

The resulting effects must pass through the same physical capability model as everything else.

A political body authorizing ten thousand additional soldiers does not create ten thousand soldiers.

It creates an organizational decision that must propagate through:

`Authorization → Recruitment / Assignment → Training → Qualification → Equipment → Logistics → Readiness → Deployment`

Likewise, funding a factory still requires construction, staffing, materials, utilities, equipment, and production.

> **Politics changes what the organization is allowed and expected to do. The world still has to do the work.**

## 8. The player's political leverage

The player can influence the galaxy at several scales.

### Personal

Influence individual leaders, advisors, commanders, scientists, merchants, and representatives.

### Organizational

Influence military units, corporations, research organizations, industrial groups, and regional administrations.

### Factional

Influence the major faction's strategic direction.

### Coalition

Coordinate several factions around shared interests.

### Galactic

Attempt to alter the balance of power among the Empire and the thirteen factions.

The player therefore has another route to power besides simply becoming the strongest military commander.

## 9. Different factions should make politics feel different

The same political action should not work identically for every faction.

Faction dispositions determine what kinds of arguments are persuasive.

Examples:

- **Veyr:** stability, order, continuity, Imperial legitimacy.
- **Cael:** competence, infrastructure, predictable administration.
- **Oris:** production, contracts, resources, industrial capacity.
- **Sere:** evidence, discovery, technological opportunity.
- **Avar:** tradition, heritage, continuity, cultural autonomy.
- **Talan:** trade, freedom, profit, mobility.
- **Merid:** consent, law, representation, public legitimacy.
- **Rhen:** independence, self-reliance, local security.
- **Solenne:** civilian welfare, dignity, protection.
- **Kharos:** strength, readiness, victory, military credibility.
- **Nyr:** sovereignty, decentralization, local control.
- **Voss:** liberation, transformation, destruction of the old order.
- **Draven:** defiance, independence, direct opposition to Imperial power.

These are tendencies, not dialogue trees.

The player should be able to surprise an audience with an argument that crosses its normal political expectations.

## 10. Political opposition is active

The player is not the only person giving speeches.

Other leaders can:

- oppose the player's proposal;
- challenge evidence;
- expose failures;
- make counter-proposals;
- recruit allies;
- threaten to withdraw support;
- call their own assembly;
- leak information;
- conduct propaganda;
- organize demonstrations;
- negotiate privately;
- attempt to replace the player.

Political conflict therefore continues while the player is elsewhere.

A political defeat should not necessarily mean combat.

The player may lose:

- funding;
- authority;
- allies;
- access;
- appointments;
- public support;
- military commitments;
- research support;
- political legitimacy.

The war continues.

## 11. Politics and espionage are connected

Political power creates information problems.

The player may need to determine:

- who actually supports the proposal;
- who is secretly loyal to the Empress;
- who is being bribed;
- who is acting for another faction;
- whether a public alliance is genuine;
- whether an apparent enemy wants negotiation;
- whether an advisor is withholding information.

Espionage can therefore support political gameplay without replacing it.

Information is useful because people make decisions with incomplete knowledge.

## 12. Assassination is a political action, not a button

Some factions may favor assassination, covert operations, intimidation, sabotage, or other forms of political violence.

These should exist as actual operations with consequences rather than as abstract faction abilities.

An assassination can alter:

- leadership;
- succession;
- faction disposition;
- alliances;
- public anger;
- military readiness;
- intelligence activity;
- Imperial intervention;
- retaliation.

Killing a leader does not automatically produce the political outcome the player wanted.

A successor may be worse.

A martyr may unify an opposition.

A faction may discover the player's involvement.

An assassination may create a war.

> **Removing a person changes a political system. It does not solve one.**

## 13. The ultimate faction capability

Each faction should eventually possess an **ultimate faction-specific capability**.

There are therefore thirteen ultimate units: one associated with each major faction.

These are not arbitrary super-units.

Each ultimate unit is the physical culmination of that faction's:

`History → Disposition → Doctrine → Research → Industry → Training → Politics → Resources → Configuration`

The unit should answer:

> **What would this society build if it concentrated its greatest strengths into one extraordinary capability?**

The thirteen ultimate units should therefore be radically different in role.

One may embody:

- overwhelming conventional warfare;

another:

- political command and influence;

another:

- espionage and intelligence;

another:

- assassination and covert action;

another:

- industrial logistics;

another:

- technological experimentation;

another:

- civilian mobilization;

another:

- frontier independence;

another:

- economic power;

another:

- revolutionary warfare.

The exact thirteen units remain **Illumination Needed**.

Their existence should not become an excuse for conventional RTS super-weapons.

The ultimate capability must still obey the world's physical and organizational rules.

## 14. Shared advanced science

Most structures should not be arbitrary faction-exclusive mysteries.

The game should establish a broad, understandable structural catalogue.

The player should know what buildings exist and what capabilities those buildings provide.

There may be a very small number of genuinely advanced science-fiction structures whose existence is initially unknown.

A candidate model is:

- **one or two shared advanced structures** that any faction can eventually research;
- **one faction-specific advanced structure/capability** associated with each faction's ultimate technological path.

This creates a clean distinction:

**Known civilization**

Most structures, facilities, utilities, factories, barracks, research facilities, logistics infrastructure, defensive works, and other physical capabilities are understood.

**Shared frontier science**

A very small number of extraordinary technologies can be independently discovered by multiple factions.

**Factional apex science**

Each faction can pursue its own extraordinary capability that reflects its identity.

The player therefore does not spend the game hunting for arbitrary missing buildings.

Instead, the unknown is concentrated where discovery is actually interesting.

## 15. Research unlocks capability; it does not erase prerequisites

Even an advanced structure must still exist physically.

The chain remains:

`Research → Design → Materials → Production / Construction → Site → Qualified personnel → Installation → Commissioning → Operation`

An ultimate unit likewise requires:

`Research → Design → Industrial capability → Components → Construction → Crew → Qualification → Logistics → Readiness → Deployment`

This preserves the central raWWar rule:

> **A technology can become possible without becoming magically available.**


## 15.1 Political research is capability research

Political research should not be reduced to a collection of percentage bonuses.

Research can expand the player's **political capability envelope**: what political actions can be performed, how frequently they can be attempted, how much preparation is possible, who can be reached, and how effectively the organization can turn political intent into action.

Examples include:

- **Rhetoric and Oratory** — improves communication with large audiences and complex arguments.
- **Audience Analysis** — reveals interests, grievances, alliances, fears, and priorities before a speech.
- **Political Organization** — improves preparation, delegation, scheduling, and coordination.
- **Public Communications** — expands communication with larger or more distant populations.
- **Coalition Building** — improves coordination among several factions.
- **Diplomatic Practice** — expands formal negotiation and alliance capabilities.
- **Political Intelligence** — improves the information available before political decisions.
- **Institutional Access** — unlocks additional political bodies, committees, councils, or levels of representation.
- **Assembly Procedures** — can unlock the ability to call or convene a faction meeting under defined circumstances.
- **Inter-Faction Diplomacy** — can unlock the ability to call a meeting involving representatives of multiple factions.
- **Galactic Congress / Summit** — a possible late-stage capability allowing the player to convene a much larger political gathering when authority, relationships, logistics, and political circumstances support it.

The important distinction is:

> **Research should unlock capabilities before it improves numbers.**

A research project might therefore change:

**Available political actions → Expanded political actions**

rather than merely:

**Speech effectiveness +10%**

Numerical improvements still have a place, but they should be consequences of acquiring better methods, information, tools, training, and institutional capability.

### Research can change political frequency

Some political actions should be limited by real organizational constraints.

A player should not simply call a faction-wide assembly every few minutes because the game needs another event.

Calling a major assembly can require:

- political authority;
- administrative staff;
- venue availability;
- preparation time;
- communications;
- agenda formation;
- supporting evidence;
- faction willingness;
- appropriate political conditions.

Research can reduce those constraints or make new procedures available.

For example:

**Political Organization research → better assembly scheduling → shorter preparation cycle → assemblies can be called more often**

Likewise:

**Public Communications research → broader communication infrastructure → more frequent / larger political addresses**

and:

**Inter-Faction Diplomacy research → formal summit procedure → multi-faction meeting becomes possible**

This is not an arbitrary cooldown reduction.

> **Research changes the machinery that makes the action possible.**

### Political research has prerequisites too

Political capability follows the same physical truth as military capability.

A research project may require:

**Research → qualified political researchers → institutional knowledge → supporting infrastructure → trained staff → procedure → capability**

A newly unlocked summit does not mean the summit magically occurs.

The player may still need:

- a place to hold it;
- staff to organize it;
- communication infrastructure;
- security;
- invitations;
- participating factions;
- transportation;
- translation or representation where applicable;
- an agenda;
- political conditions that make attendance possible.

This lets political research become another expression of the raWWar capability model:

> **Research determines what can become possible. Organizations, people, infrastructure, resources, and procedures determine whether it can happen now.**

### Political research should be faction-shaped

The same research domain can produce different capabilities in different factions.

A militaristic faction may research methods for rallying soldiers and securing wartime support.

A mercantile faction may develop sophisticated negotiation, contract, and trade-congress procedures.

A revolutionary faction may develop clandestine organizing and mass mobilization.

A highly centralized faction may develop enormous formal assemblies while a decentralized faction develops distributed councils.

Thus:

**Faction history + disposition + institutions + research → political capability**

Political research becomes another place where faction identity emerges from the society rather than from arbitrary ability lists.

## 16. Politics as another configuration problem

The player is effectively configuring political relationships just as they configure:

- vehicles;
- exoskeletons;
- factories;
- bases;
- logistics networks;
- research programs;
- military formations.

The difference is that political components are people and organizations.

A useful abstraction is:

`People + Interests + Information + Reputation + Intent + Relationships → Political Outcome`

The player experiments.

The galaxy responds.

The player learns.

The player tries again under different circumstances.

There is no universal political build order.

## 17. No political victory screen

A successful speech does not end the game.

A successful alliance does not end the game.

A successful coalition does not end the game.

Even a successful attempt to defeat the Empress should not necessarily produce a conventional congratulations screen.

Political success changes the world.

The player then has to live with the new world.

> **You did not win politics. You changed what politics is about.**

## 18. Design principles

1. Politics is first-person gameplay.
2. Speeches are physical Experiences, not abstract menus.
3. The audience consists of people and organizations with interests.
4. Reputation and history matter.
5. Alliances create obligations.
6. Political authorization still has to propagate through physical capability.
7. Opposition is active.
8. Espionage supplies information but does not replace political interaction.
9. Assassination changes systems and creates consequences.
10. Each faction should provide a distinct political experience.
11. Each faction eventually has one ultimate faction-specific unit/capability.
12. Most structures are known and understandable.
13. Only a very small number of advanced structures should remain genuinely mysterious.
14. Shared advanced science can be discovered by multiple factions.
15. Faction-specific apex science should reflect faction identity.
16. Political success changes the living galaxy rather than ending it.
