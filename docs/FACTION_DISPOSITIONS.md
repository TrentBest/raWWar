# raWWar — Faction Dispositions

**Status:** Living design model.  
**Purpose:** Translate faction history into behavioral tendencies that can influence diplomacy, policy, military behavior, recruitment, research, logistics, and sub-faction behavior.

> **A faction should behave according to what it values, not according to a label that says what it is.**

---

## 1. Dispositions are not morality

A disposition is a tendency.

It is not:

- a good/evil score;
- a player alignment;
- a permanent rule;
- a deterministic prediction of every decision.

A faction can have a high value-of-life disposition and still order soldiers into terrible battles.

A faction can value individual freedom while exploiting workers.

A faction can oppose the Empress while cooperating with her.

A faction can value stability while causing enormous suffering in the name of preserving it.

The numbers describe **what pressures the faction is likely to feel when making decisions**.

---

## 2. Core disposition dimensions

The initial model uses a 0–100 scale.

### Imperial Alignment

**0** = existentially opposed to Imperial authority  
**50** = transactional / situational  
**100** = strongly supportive of Imperial authority

This is the hidden political dimension behind the thirteen-door ordering.

It is **not shown to the player as a number**.

### Value of Life

**0** = lives are highly expendable  
**50** = lives have conditional strategic value  
**100** = preservation of life is a major political priority

This does not mean high values always produce pacifism.

A faction may value life so strongly that it becomes willing to use extreme force to prevent civilian casualties.

### Individual Freedom

**0** = individual choice is subordinate to collective authority  
**50** = freedom balanced against institutional needs  
**100** = individual autonomy is a primary value

### Centralization

**0** = power should remain local  
**50** = shared authority  
**100** = strong centralized authority

### Tradition

**0** = inherited practice has little authority  
**50** = tradition is one influence among many  
**100** = inherited institutions and customs are highly authoritative

### Innovation

**0** = proven methods preferred  
**50** = selective innovation  
**100** = experimentation and technological change strongly preferred

### Commerce

**0** = markets are subordinate to political/social objectives  
**50** = mixed motivation  
**100** = trade, wealth, and economic freedom are major priorities

### Military Orientation

**0** = military force is primarily defensive or reluctant  
**50** = professional military capability is accepted  
**100** = military strength is central to political identity

### Civilian Protection

**0** = civilian welfare is secondary to strategic objectives  
**50** = protection is situational  
**100** = civilian protection is a primary operational concern

This differs from Value of Life.

A faction can value life abstractly while treating enemy civilians as strategically irrelevant.

### Risk Tolerance

**0** = strongly risk-averse  
**50** = balanced  
**100** = highly willing to accept uncertainty and loss

### Pragmatism

**0** = ideology/principle dominates expediency  
**50** = principle and practicality compete  
**100** = outcomes strongly outweigh ideological consistency

### Coercion Tolerance

**0** = coercion is highly disfavored  
**50** = coercion accepted under defined circumstances  
**100** = coercion is a normal political instrument

### Collective Duty

**0** = personal interest dominates  
**50** = personal and collective obligations are balanced  
**100** = duty to organization/community/state dominates

# 4. Why the ratings matter

The numbers should eventually influence actual behavior.

They should not simply appear in a faction-selection screen.

For example:

### High Imperial Alignment

More likely to:

- accept Imperial contracts;
- provide troops to Imperial campaigns;
- negotiate before resisting;
- protect Imperial institutions;
- accept Imperial standards;
- receive Imperial patronage;
- compete for Imperial favor.

### High Value of Life

More likely to:

- invest in medical infrastructure;
- avoid unnecessary casualties;
- evacuate wounded personnel;
- preserve experienced crews;
- value rescue operations;
- develop safer procedures;
- object to deliberately expendable operations.

### High Individual Freedom

More likely to:

- tolerate local autonomy;
- permit independent organizations;
- encourage voluntary association;
- resist mandatory political control;
- allow internal dissent.

### High Centralization

More likely to:

- standardize equipment;
- centralize logistics;
- enforce common procedures;
- maintain large administrative systems;
- issue centralized orders;
- suppress local deviation when it threatens policy.

### High Innovation

More likely to:

- fund research;
- accept experimental equipment;
- tolerate prototype failure;
- retrain personnel;
- replace established systems;
- pursue technological advantage.

### High Commerce

More likely to:

- establish trade routes;
- protect merchant shipping;
- negotiate contracts;
- exploit price differences;
- invest in logistics;
- tolerate politically inconvenient trading partners.

### High Military Orientation

More likely to:

- maintain larger standing forces;
- train more aggressively;
- prioritize readiness;
- invest in weapons;
- develop military infrastructure;
- interpret political problems through strategic power.

### High Civilian Protection

More likely to:

- preserve civilian infrastructure;
- evacuate populations;
- establish protected zones;
- restrict fire support;
- spend resources on humanitarian operations;
- consider civilian casualties strategically important.

### High Risk Tolerance

More likely to:

- attempt unconventional operations;
- accept incomplete information;
- use experimental technology;
- exploit temporary opportunities;
- conduct raids and deep operations.

### High Pragmatism

More likely to:

- make temporary alliances;
- trade with enemies;
- compromise ideology;
- accept politically uncomfortable solutions;
- exploit contradictions.

### High Coercion Tolerance

More likely to:

- impose martial law;
- use detention;
- threaten noncompliant organizations;
- enforce resource allocation;
- suppress dissent;
- use force before negotiation.

### High Collective Duty

More likely to:

- sacrifice individual preference for mission;
- maintain discipline;
- reward service;
- expect obedience;
- preserve institutional continuity.

# 5. Dispositions interact

The most interesting behavior should come from combinations.

A single rating rarely explains a decision.

Consider:

**High Value of Life + High Military Orientation**

This does not produce pacifism.

It can produce:

> **Extremely well-trained soldiers, excellent medical support, and a refusal to waste experienced personnel.**

Consider:

**High Freedom + High Commerce**

This can produce:

> **Aggressive free markets, private security, independent merchants, and weak willingness to accept regulation.**

Consider:

**High Innovation + High Coercion**

This can produce:

> **A technologically advanced faction willing to conduct dangerous experiments on unwilling subjects.**

Consider:

**High Tradition + High Value of Life**

This can produce:

> **A deeply conservative society that nevertheless protects its people fiercely.**

Consider:

**High Pragmatism + Low Imperial Alignment**

This can produce:

> **A faction that hates the Empire but still accepts an Imperial contract when doing so prevents a worse outcome.**

This is where the factions stop feeling like alignment colors.

# 6. Historical disposition inheritance

Sub-factions should inherit dispositions from their parent faction as tendencies.

They can then drift.

A local world might become:

- more militaristic after generations of invasion;
- more humanitarian after surviving a massacre;
- more pro-Imperial after receiving Imperial protection;
- more anti-Imperial after an Imperial occupation;
- more innovative after discovering a unique resource;
- more isolationist after losing its trade routes;
- more coercive after repeated internal rebellion.

Conceptually:

**Parent disposition**
→ **local history**
→ **local leadership**
→ **local population**
→ **local threats**
→ **local resources**
→ **local experience**
→ **sub-faction disposition**

This is critical.

A Rhen world should not simply be:

Rhen = 22 Imperial

It should be possible for a particular Rhen world to become considerably more Imperial after generations of protection.

Likewise, a Veyr colony could become openly rebellious if Imperial policies repeatedly destroy the local population.

---

# 7. Dispositions should influence capability

The existing capability matrix answers:

> **What is this faction likely to be capable of?**

The disposition matrix helps answer:

> **Why would they invest in it?**

For example:

**Military Orientation + Collective Duty + Centralization**

can explain:

- large standing armies;
- standardized equipment;
- formal training;
- centralized logistics.

**Innovation + Risk + Research**

can explain:

- experimental aircraft;
- prototype weapons;
- advanced sensors;
- unusual research facilities.

**Commerce + Freedom + Pragmatism**

can explain:

- merchant fleets;
- private security;
- flexible logistics;
- mixed equipment;
- foreign technology acquisition.

**Value of Life + Civilian Protection**

can explain:

- medical facilities;
- rescue craft;
- evacuation capability;
- protected infrastructure;
- investment in survivability.

The capability matrix and disposition matrix therefore complement each other.

# 8. Dispositions should influence relationships

Diplomacy can compare dispositions as well as historical events.

Two factions with similar values may cooperate even if their interests differ.

Two factions with incompatible values may cooperate temporarily if their interests overlap.

Examples:

### Merid + Solenne

Potentially strong compatibility around:

- civilian protection;
- legitimacy;
- infrastructure;
- representation.

Potential conflict:

- Merid may prioritize legal process;
- Solenne may prioritize immediate humanitarian action.

### Talan + Oris

Potentially strong compatibility around:

- production;
- trade;
- logistics;
- wealth.

Potential conflict:

- Oris may favor industrial control;
- Talan may resist economic monopolies.

### Kharos + Veyr

Potential compatibility around:

- discipline;
- military readiness;
- hierarchy;
- stability.

Potential conflict:

- Veyr accepts Imperial authority;
- Kharos wants military competence to remain politically independent.

### Voss + Draven

Potential compatibility around:

- opposition to the Empire;
- aggressive action;
- revolutionary pressure.

Potential conflict:

- Voss wants transformation;
- Draven wants destruction of the Imperial order;
- both may disagree violently about what comes afterward.

# 9. Disposition drift

A faction's disposition should be capable of changing.

The historical record should matter.

Repeated events can move values gradually.

Examples:

**Repeated Imperial aid**
→ increased Imperial trust.

**Imperial betrayal**
→ decreased Imperial trust.

**Civilian massacre**
→ increased hostility toward coercive factions.

**Successful technological breakthrough**
→ increased support for innovation.

**Technological catastrophe**
→ increased caution.

**Repeated invasion**
→ increased military orientation.

**Long peace**
→ reduced military priority.

**Economic collapse**
→ increased centralization or increased localism depending on political response.

**Successful trade liberalization**
→ increased support for commerce/freedom.

**Internal rebellion**
→ increased coercion or increased decentralization depending on who wins.

The faction therefore has a history of changing its mind.

---

# 10. The player can change faction disposition

The player should not merely inherit a faction's values.

The player can become a historical force.

A commander who repeatedly:

- saves civilians;
- preserves enemy wounded;
- protects soldiers;
- refuses politically convenient atrocities;
- rewards competence;
- invests in training;

may gradually influence the faction.

Likewise, a commander who repeatedly:

- sacrifices personnel;
- suppresses dissent;
- centralizes authority;
- rewards aggression;
- ignores civilian casualties;

can move the local organization in another direction.

This should be especially strong at the sub-faction level.

The player may not be able to rewrite an entire major faction immediately.

They can change the world directly around them.

---

# 11. The Empress is also a disposition-bearing actor

The Empress should not merely be:

Imperial Alignment = 100

She needs her own political personality.

Her eventual disposition model can include:

- need for control;
- tolerance for dissent;
- value of life;
- strategic patience;
- political pragmatism;
- appetite for risk;
- trust;
- paranoia;
- desire for legacy;
- willingness to delegate;
- willingness to forgive;
- willingness to punish.

This allows the same Imperial office to behave differently depending on the Empress and historical period.

---

# 12. Ratings are simulation inputs, not UI decoration

The final implementation should not reduce the model to a single set of labels.

The values should participate in decisions.

Conceptually:

**Situation**
+
**Historical memory**
+
**Current relationships**
+
**Disposition**
+
**Available capabilities**
+
**Resources**
+
**Leadership**
+
**Risk**
→
**Candidate actions**
→
**Decision procedure**
→
**FSM execution**
→
**Historical consequence**

The disposition narrows and weights choices.

It does not replace the decision procedure.

---

# 13. The important distinction

There are now three useful layers:

### Capability

**Can we do it?**

Technology, production, infrastructure, personnel, qualifications, equipment, logistics.

### Disposition

**Are we inclined to do it?**

Values, attitudes, risk tolerance, political philosophy, cultural memory.

### Situation

**Does it make sense to do it now?**

Threats, resources, relationships, opportunities, orders, history, geography.

Together:

> **Capability says what can happen. Disposition says what is likely to be chosen. Situation says what makes sense right now.**

---

# 14. The resulting faction model

The faction is therefore no longer merely:

Faction
→ units
→ buildings
→ vehicles

It becomes:

HISTORY
↓
COLLECTIVE MEMORY
↓
DISPOSITIONS
↙    ↓     ↘
VALUES   FEARS   DESIRES
↓       ↓       ↓
DOCTRINE / POLITICS
↓
CAPABILITY TENDENCIES
↓
LOCAL SUB-FACTIONS
↓
ACTUAL CAPABILITIES
↓
SITUATION
↓
DECISION PROCESS
↓
FSMs / WORK
↓
CONSEQUENCES
↓
HISTORY

That loop is the important part.

**History creates disposition.**

**Disposition influences decisions.**

**Decisions create history.**

---

## 15. Design principle

> **A faction is not a color, a technology tree, or a list of units. A faction is a population with a history that has taught it what to value, what to fear, and what it is willing to do.**

That is what should make thirteen factions feel like thirteen civilizations rather than thirteen skins.
