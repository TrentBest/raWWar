# raWWar — Capability Catalogue: Vehicles, Structures, Factories & Qualifications

Status: Living design catalogue.

> **This is the catalogue of things the player can eventually build, qualify for, operate, staff, maintain, research, manufacture, and command.**

This document is deliberately broader than a conventional unit list. In raWWar, a vehicle is not merely a unit, a building is not merely a construction option, and a factory is not merely a production button.

They are physical capabilities embedded in an organization.

A capability becomes useful only when the organization can provide the people, qualifications, equipment, resources, procedures, and readiness required to use it.

---

## 1. The central model

The core dependency is:

```
DESIRED CAPABILITY
      ↓
physical structure / vehicle / facility
      ↓
required crew
      ↓
required squads
      ↓
required qualifications
      ↓
training capacity
      ↓
barracks + training modules
      ↓
qualified people
      ↓
staffing + readiness
      ↓
CAPABILITY AVAILABLE
```

For manufacturing:

```
PRODUCT
  ↓
factory type
  ↓
factory equipment
  ↓
factory crew
  ↓
factory qualifications
  ↓
trained personnel
  ↓
materials + power + tooling
  ↓
production
  ↓
vehicle/equipment
  ↓
operational crew
  ↓
field use
```

The important consequence is:

> **A player cannot solve every problem by constructing another building. Eventually the bottleneck becomes people.**

# 2. Training is infrastructure

A standard barracks contains **four VR training modules**.

That gives the barracks a concrete organizational meaning:

- one squad can train at a time;
- a squad is four people;
- four modules correspond to the four simultaneous trainees;
- the barracks therefore represent one squad-sized training pipeline.

Training capacity is consequently a strategic resource.

A new vehicle may require three squads.

A new facility may require five squads.

A major factory may require dozens of qualified people across several specialties.

The player therefore has to expand the **human infrastructure** of the base as capabilities become more complicated.

## 2.1 Training tiers

The intended model is a chained qualification ladder inspired by the useful part of tiered training systems such as *Evil Genius*, but presented through raWWar's physical military world.

A trainee cannot simply purchase Tier 4.

They must possess the prerequisite qualification from the tier below.

Candidate ladder:

| Tier | Meaning | Typical result |
|---|---|---|
| T0 | Unqualified | General labor / observation / basic duties |
| T1 | Basic | Entry-level military or technical operation |
| T2 | Qualified | Independent operation of ordinary equipment |
| T3 | Advanced | Complex systems and specialized crews |
| T4 | Expert | High-value, dangerous, or technically demanding systems |
| T5 | Master / Instructor | Advanced command, instruction, certification, or rare systems |

This is a **qualification ladder**, not a fixed character class.

A soldier can climb different ladders in different domains.

A person could therefore be:

- T4 pilot;
- T2 mechanic;
- T3 construction specialist;
- T1 research assistant;
- T0 medical.

The player is building a person, not selecting a class.

## 2.2 Training is itself a procedure

Training occupies real physical infrastructure.

A training session has:

- trainees;
- instructor(s);
- training module(s);
- curriculum;
- duration;
- prerequisites;
- equipment;
- facility requirements;
- completion criteria;
- failure/recovery conditions;
- resulting qualification.

The base should visibly contain training activity.

There should be drills everywhere.

Soldiers march.

Squads form up.

Instructors demonstrate.

People run exercises.

Vehicles are used for training.

The base should look like an enormous military organization continuously preparing itself for war.

### Strategic transport principle

A major invasion should not require the player to personally drive every vehicle across enormous distances.

The player can move forces by air.

A dedicated heavy VTOL/carryall can:

- load vehicles;
- load troops;
- transport them over terrain;
- approach the target;
- survive or evade enemy air defense;
- deploy vehicles;
- deploy troops;
- return or remain on station.

The vehicle itself becomes a capability chain:

```
Transport capacity
→ trained flight crew
→ trained loading crew
→ compatible cargo
→ flight readiness
→ route
→ enemy AA threat
→ survivability
→ deployment procedure
→ battlefield arrival
```

Upgrades can change the actual operation:

- stronger thrusters;
- greater lift;
- larger cargo capacity;
- faster deployment;
- better armor;
- improved countermeasures;
- better sensors;

# 9. Factory crews

Factories require trained people just as vehicles do.

A factory may require:

- operators;
- technicians;
- engineers;
- quality-control personnel;
- maintenance personnel;
- supervisors;
- logistics staff;
- safety staff;
- security.

A factory can physically exist and still be incapable of producing because it lacks qualified staff.

This creates the same fundamental rule:

> **Capacity is not readiness. Readiness is not capability. Capability is not availability.**

A factory with ten assembly stations but only one qualified crew is not a ten-station production miracle.

# 11. Research as 0–100 capability

The proposed research model is:

> **Every research subject has a research value from 0 to 100.**

Research funding/progress cannot exceed 100.

Once a technology reaches 100, additional research funding cannot simply increase the same value.

This is important because 100 represents **completion of the currently defined research generation**, not the end of technological evolution.

The next improvement can become a new research subject, generation, branch, or application.

## Example: PlasmaCrete

```
PlasmaCrete Armor
0 ─────────────────────────────────────── 100
                ↑
               30
                │
                └─ lightweight PlasmaCrete research unlocks
```

At 0–29:

- the material exists as a concept/capability;
- the player may investigate its properties;
- advanced optimization remains unavailable.

At 30:

- a new research branch becomes available;
- the player can begin reducing weight without sacrificing the established performance target.

At higher thresholds, additional branches may unlock:

- manufacturing efficiency;
- improved durability;
- reduced mass;
- thermal performance;
- impact resistance;
- flexibility;
- cheaper production;
- improved repairability.

The important rule is:

> **Research level unlocks what can be investigated next. It does not simply add arbitrary +1 bonuses.**

---

# 12. Research thresholds

A technology can therefore have a dependency graph:

```
PlasmaCrete Armor
       │
       ├── 30 → Lightweight formulation
       │
       ├── 45 → Improved manufacturing process
       │
       ├── 60 → Flexible armor application
       │
       ├── 75 → Advanced thermal protection
       │
       └── 90 → Next-generation material platform
```

The exact thresholds should vary by technology.

We should avoid making every technology use the same unlock pattern.

Research should create discoveries that make technological sense.

# 13. Research, production, use

The three progression layers remain distinct.

### Research

Determines:

- what is understood;
- what can be developed;
- what variants can be attempted;
- what technological branches are unlocked.

### Production

Determines:

- what can actually be manufactured;
- quantity;
- quality;
- cost;
- throughput;
- reliability;
- yield;
- maintainability.

### Use

Determines:

- who receives it;
- who can operate it;
- who can maintain it;
- what configuration they use;
- what they actually experience.

Therefore:

> **Research defines what can be built → production defines what can be fielded → use defines what the soldier experiences.**

  ↓

  ↓
Plan operation
  ↓
Stage forces
  ↓
Move forces
  ↓
Maintain defensive position
  ↓

  ↓

## 14.1 Strategic movement

The player does not have to personally drive every kilometer.

Units can be transported by aircraft/VTOL.

A heavy transport VTOL can carry:

- soldiers;
- vehicles;
- equipment;
- supplies.

It can deploy those assets directly into the operational area.

## 14.2 Air defense becomes meaningful

The transport aircraft is itself a strategic capability.

Enemy AA can:

- detect it;
- track it;
- engage it;
- force route changes;
- delay deployment;
- damage it;
- destroy it.

The player's transport technology can evolve through:

- armor;
- thrusters;
- lift;
- speed;
- sensors;
- countermeasures;
- communications;
- deployment mechanisms;
- cargo capacity;
- survivability.

This means an apparently simple requirement—"get my troops there"—can become an entire military operation.

# 16. Qualification should explain the world

When the player encounters a locked capability, the answer should not merely be:

> "Requires Level 4."

The world should be able to explain:

> "This vehicle requires two flight crews, one maintenance squad, and a specialized weapons technician. Your base currently has one qualified flight crew and no weapons technicians. The training wing can begin the required qualification, but the current barracks are already occupied."

That is a much more useful game decision.

It tells the player:

- what is missing;
- why it is missing;
- what can fix it;
- what the bottleneck is;
- what the consequence will be.

The same information can be presented by:

- advisor;
- administrator;
- instructor;
- tablet;
- terminal;
- physical observation;
- direct interaction.

The underlying data remains authoritative.

# 18. Design rule: catalogue families, not a frozen spreadsheet

We should not prematurely create 2,000 individually named vehicles.

The important first layer is:

1. capability family;
2. physical manifestation;
3. crew requirement;
4. qualification requirement;
5. training requirement;
6. factory requirement;
7. research dependencies;
8. production dependencies;
9. operational procedure;
10. upgrade/research branches.

Specific named vehicles can then emerge from those families.

This is important because raWWar's technology system is intended to allow variation.

A player's fighter does not need to be identical to every other faction's fighter.

A faction can have:

- different materials;
- different propulsion;
- different sensors;
- different armor;
- different production quality;
- different research;
- different manufacturing doctrine.

The same capability family can therefore produce many actual vehicles.

# 19. Immediate catalogue-building order

The next useful design passes should be:

### Pass 1 — Core qualifications
Define the qualification families and T0–T5 progression.

### Pass 2 — Base
Define every structure needed to establish a functioning military base.

### Pass 3 — Industrial chain
Define factories and what they require from one another.

### Pass 4 — Vehicles
Define ground, atmospheric, orbital, and strategic vehicle families.

### Pass 5 — Crew compositions
For every important vehicle/facility, define the required squad count and specialties.

### Pass 6 — Research subjects
Turn the technology universe into 0–100 research domains.

### Pass 7 — Unlock thresholds
Define the research thresholds that expose new technology branches.

### Pass 8 — Invasion logistics
Connect intelligence → staging → transport → AA → deployment → assault.

### Pass 9 — Living-base activity
Define what personnel are physically doing when the player is not directly interacting with them.

### Pass 10 — Named content
Only after the capability network works should we start naming individual vehicles, buildings, weapons, and technologies.

---

# 20. Ultimate rule

> **Everything the player can do should have a corresponding capability in the world.**

If the player can operate a vehicle, someone had to build it.

If someone built it, a factory had to make it.

If the factory made it, people had to operate the factory.

If those people can operate the factory, they had to be trained.

If they had to be trained, the base needed training capacity.

If the vehicle exists because of a technological advance, someone researched it.

If the player wants to deploy it, logistics must move it.

If the enemy can stop it, intelligence and defense matter.

And when the player finally arrives at the battlefield:

**the vehicle, the crew, the training, the factory, the research, the logistics, and the decision that created it all arrive with them.**

That is raWWar.
