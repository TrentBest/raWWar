## Scope, status, and relationship to the Nacrean case study

This is a **Fictional** LOD 3 setting volume. Pelagos, the Serein Passage, the neighboring societies, all named institutions, treaties, events, and characters are invented. No extraterrestrial civilization or inhabited exoplanet has been confirmed. Broad physical principles such as pressure, fluid motion, energy budgets, heat transfer, material limits, and communication delay are **Established**; the particular moon, currents, technologies, and political histories are **Fictional** setting premises.

This book expands [The Nacrean Compact and the Tidal Archive Crisis](nacrean-compact-and-tidal-archive-crisis.md) from one polity and one constitutional crisis into a connected regional theatre. It does not replace that volume's account. It supplies the neighboring actors, passage agreements, shared systems, and prior disputes that make the Nacrean crisis matter beyond its own borders.

## 1. Regional theatre: the Serein basin

The Serein basin is a broad, fictional ocean region on Pelagos. Three major current systems meet across a chain of shelves, volcanic ridges, and deep-water channels. The currents move nutrients and floating material, but they also create maintenance windows, navigation hazards, and uneven access to energy and food. A route that is efficient in one season may become expensive or unsafe in another.

The word *passage* refers not to one narrow strait but to a maintained network: pressure-rated transfer stations, marked current corridors, relay buoys, repair platforms, refuge habitats, and agreed emergency channels. The passage is therefore simultaneously a geographic route, a public service, a legal arrangement, and a political relationship.

### The three currents

- **The Serein Drift** is the relatively predictable north-eastern flow used by scheduled freight and passenger convoys. Its predictability makes it commercially valuable and politically visible.
- **The Vey Turning** is a seasonal circulation that carries food cultures, larvae, and migration craft between shallow shelves. It is productive but variable; local navigators possess knowledge that central route tables often understate.
- **The Ashfall Return** is a deeper, slower current near geothermal ridges. It supports specialist transport and energy infrastructure, but its thermal and chemical variation makes maintenance expensive.

These are not fixed lanes on a map. They are changing physical systems. Any campaign using them should make season, local conditions, vessel capability, and maintenance status matter.

### Regional geography and dependencies

The basin contains five broad zones:

1. **Nacrean Shelf and Reef Arc:** the Compact's distributed settlements, reef gardens, archive facilities, and inter-settlement relay network.
2. **Vey Shallows:** a mosaic of inhabited shelves and mobile cultivation platforms whose communities depend on seasonal exchange and local navigation.
3. **Orison Thermal Rim:** geothermal works, ceramic fabrication, pressure-component workshops, and hazardous maintenance stations.
4. **The Serein Transfer Chain:** shared refuges, convoy rendezvous platforms, medical transfer points, and relay buoys governed through treaty arrangements.
5. **The Outer Drift Communities:** mobile and semi-mobile flotillas, independent repair crews, family networks, and seasonal workers whose legal status varies across jurisdictions.

A regional map should show jurisdiction, customary use, ecological zones, service coverage, and travel-time bands as separate layers. Do not draw a single border and imply it answers every question.

## 2. Neighboring political societies

The region contains more than three flags. These profiles identify major institutions, not unified personalities. Each polity contains competing constituencies, local authorities, and people who disagree about its future.

### The Nacrean Compact

The Compact is a distributed constitutional polity, not a unitary ocean-wide state. Its settlements retain local authority over many services while delegating defined responsibilities to Compact institutions. Political membership is layered: residents, chartered communities, protected dependents, mobile residents, and treaty-recognized visitors do not all possess identical rights.

The Compact's central disputes concern representation, archive provenance, emergency powers, and the balance between local autonomy and shared infrastructure. The Tidal Archive Crisis exposed how an apparently technical records dispute could alter treaty obligations and public trust across the basin.

### The Vey Shelf Assemblies

The Vey Shelf Assemblies are a confederation of settlement councils, cultivation cooperatives, navigation houses, and seasonal gathering forums. Their authority is negotiated and overlapping. A council may control a platform's maintenance budget while a navigation house controls customary route knowledge and a cooperative owns the equipment.

Internal currents:
- **Continuity delegates** prioritize predictable harvest and passage schedules.
- **Local-water advocates** resist decisions that treat customary routes as centrally assignable property.
- **Open-exchange cooperatives** favor broader access for traders, migrants, and independent repair crews.
- **Stewardship circles** seek ecological limits and slower infrastructure expansion.
- **Young platform councils** argue that older agreements underrepresent newer settlements and mobile households.

These are shifting coalitions, not permanent parties. A person may support open exchange while opposing a particular concession.

### The Orison Works League

The Orison Works League coordinates workshops, thermal stations, specialist crews, and material exchanges around the geothermal rim. It is neither simply a corporation nor a conventional state. Its constituent workshops have different ownership and labor arrangements; public utilities, worker trusts, family firms, and cooperative fabrication halls coexist.

Internal currents:
- **Reliability engineers** want stable maintenance funding and transparent component standards.
- **Workshop autonomists** resist external control of production schedules.
- **Public-service stewards** argue that critical pressure components should be governed as regional utilities.
- **Expansion syndicates** seek new contracts and larger fabrication capacity.
- **Labor delegates** focus on safety, training, injury compensation, and bargaining power.

The League's leverage comes from scarce expertise and difficult-to-replace manufacturing capacity, not from an inherent desire to dominate its neighbors.

### The Outer Drift Mutuals

The Outer Drift Mutuals are a loose network of flotilla councils, repair crews, family associations, and mobile service providers. They have no single capital or executive. Some members are long-established residents; others are seasonal workers or people displaced by habitat failures and political exclusion.

The Mutuals coordinate rescue signals, shared maintenance caches, dispute mediation, and passage information. They disagree over whether to seek formal treaty recognition, preserve flexible independence, or negotiate recognition separately with each polity. Their members should not be treated as one fleet, one culture, or a ready-made insurgency.

### The Serein Passage Commission

The Commission is a treaty-created administrative body, not a sovereign government. It maintains shared route markers, refuge stations, relay schedules, incident reporting, and a public maintenance ledger. Its board includes delegates from the Compact, Vey Assemblies, Orison League, and Outer Drift observers. The observers' authority is narrower than that of treaty signatories, a persistent source of dispute.

The Commission can coordinate and publish; it cannot lawfully command every vessel or seize every facility. Its legitimacy depends on accurate records, fair service allocation, and the confidence that emergency rules will not become permanent political control.

## 3. The passage treaties

The Serein agreements developed in stages after a series of avoidable transport emergencies. The treaty system is a layered compact rather than a single founding document.

### The Refuge and Rescue Accord

The first agreement guarantees distress recognition, emergency refuge, medical transfer, and non-discriminatory rescue irrespective of a vessel's commercial or political status. It does not settle long-term residence or immigration status. Rescue is not a grant of citizenship, and a rescued person cannot be treated as property or as a bargaining token.

### The Shared Markers Protocol

The second agreement establishes common route-marker conventions, maintenance reporting, incident logs, and a schedule for replacing damaged buoys. It requires provenance for data corrections and preserves local observations alongside standardized measurements. This matters because a centralized map can be internally consistent and still wrong for a particular season or community.

### The Passage and Fair Access Treaty

The third agreement defines access windows, convoy priority categories, toll principles, appeals, and protected transit for medical and essential-service traffic. It distinguishes:
- routine commercial passage;
- essential food, medicine, and life-support components;
- rescue and evacuation traffic;
- scientific and ecological monitoring;
- maintenance and repair traffic;
- temporary closures for immediate safety.

The treaty does not guarantee that every vessel can travel at every time. It requires that restrictions be evidence-based, published where possible, proportionate, reviewable, and time-limited.

### The Thermal Works and Materials Compact

This agreement coordinates access to Orison's specialist fabrication and thermal facilities. It sets reciprocal maintenance obligations and defines how scarce pressure-rated components are allocated during shortages. It also recognizes the League's workers and workshop communities as participants in decisions that affect their safety and livelihood.

### The Records and Provenance Schedule

The most recent schedule links route-marker data, maintenance logs, incident records, and public notices. It requires signed corrections, version history, independent audit, and a way to preserve minority observations. The Tidal Archive Crisis revealed that record integrity is not a clerical concern: a disputed model can change who appears to have violated a treaty, which communities receive compensation, and which routes are considered safe.

## 4. Trade, migration, and regional livelihoods

Trade is not merely a flow of abstract resources. Every exchange depends on people, vessels, storage, training, repair, legal recognition, and the ability to absorb delays.

### Major exchanges

| Flow | Principal sources | Principal uses | Strategic sensitivity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cultivated food cultures | Vey shelves, Nacrean reef gardens | Regional food security | Seasonal variation and disease control |
| Pressure-rated ceramics | Orison workshops | Habitat repair, transfer stations | Specialist capacity and quality assurance |
| Relay components | Orison, Compact fabrication halls | Navigation and public communications | Maintenance backlogs and standards |
| Medical cultures and supplies | Distributed clinics and research stations | Rescue, routine care, outbreak response | Cold-chain and access |
| Energy storage media | Multiple geothermal and tidal facilities | Mobile power, emergency reserves | Material scarcity and recharge time |
| Navigation and ecological knowledge | Local pilots, researchers, Outer Drift crews | Safe passage and stewardship | Attribution, compensation, and trust |

A campaign should avoid making any one resource a magical single point of failure. The more interesting question is often how substitution, repair, rationing, and political bargaining work over time.

### Migration and residence

Movement through the basin includes seasonal work, education, family reunification, trade, medical transfer, ecological relocation, and permanent resettlement. These categories overlap. People may move because they want opportunity, because a platform is being repaired, because a harvest has failed, or because they no longer feel safe under a local authority.

The treaty system recognizes emergency refuge more clearly than it recognizes long-term mobility. This gap produces recurring disputes:
- Who may remain after a rescue?
- Which authority is responsible for schooling and medical care?
- Can mobile workers vote in the jurisdiction where they live most of the year?
- Who owns or maintains equipment left behind during an evacuation?
- Can a settlement refuse new arrivals while still relying on their labor?

Do not turn migrants into a faceless pressure wave. Give individuals relationships, skills, obligations, and different views about where they belong.

## 5. Shared infrastructure and civilian continuity

The passage is a network of maintained services. Its security is best understood through continuity, accountability, and repair—not through a checklist of targets.

### Critical service families

- **Navigation:** route markers, current observations, charts, pilot training, and incident notices.
- **Communications:** local mesh links, relay schedules, emergency channels, and physical message carriage.
- **Refuge:** pressure-rated shelters, transfer platforms, food reserves, medical capacity, and accessible evacuation procedures.
- **Fabrication:** component standards, testing, repair crews, spare inventories, and training pipelines.
- **Ecological monitoring:** water quality, food-culture health, current changes, and habitat impact.
- **Records:** public notices, treaty versions, maintenance history, incident evidence, and independent audit.

For each service, record ownership, operator, regulator, funding source, maintenance responsibility, users, backup arrangements, and appeal path. A shared asset can have multiple legitimate authorities.

### Resilience principles

1. Preserve service while investigating responsibility whenever safety permits.
2. Separate observation, interpretation, and political judgment in incident records.
3. Maintain independent backups and auditable correction histories.
4. Design refuge and evacuation capacity around actual populations, including people with limited mobility and visitors.
5. Publish the reason, scope, and review time for emergency restrictions.
6. Compensate the workers who perform hazardous maintenance.
7. Do not assume redundancy exists merely because a diagram shows two lines; test whether the alternate path has capacity, trained staff, and compatible equipment.

This book deliberately does not provide procedures for sabotaging real infrastructure. It focuses on governance, safety, resilience, and the political consequences of service failure.

## 6. Historical development of the regional order

### The Unmarked Years

Before formal passage agreements, communities relied on local route knowledge, kinship networks, and informal rescue obligations. Trade was possible, but unfamiliar vessels faced inconsistent rules and uncertain refuge access. The historical record is fragmentary because many early settlements maintained separate calendars and oral incident traditions.

### The Three Rescue Seasons

A sequence of three severe seasonal emergencies exposed gaps in distress recognition and transfer capacity. No single event created the treaty system; repeated local improvisation did. Rescue crews from rival communities became trusted intermediaries, while arguments over costs and jurisdiction continued.

### The First Refuge Accord

The first treaty formalized rescue and refuge duties. Its narrow scope made agreement possible: it did not resolve borders, tolls, or permanent migration. Later political narratives sometimes portray it as a moment of regional unity, but contemporary accounts show disputes over compensation and enforcement.

### The Marker Dispute

A set of conflicting current observations led to incompatible route notices. The disagreement was initially technical, then became political when commercial losses and delayed evacuations were attributed to different versions of the route record. The Shared Markers Protocol created a common standard while preserving local observations.

### The Passage Settlement

The Passage and Fair Access Treaty created the Commission and the first shared allocation schedule. It reduced uncertainty but introduced new arguments about representation, tolls, and who counted as an affected community. The Outer Drift Mutuals gained observer status but not full signatory power.

### The Thermal Works Compact

A shortage of specialist components delayed multiple repairs. The resulting negotiations recognized Orison's industrial capacity as a regional dependency and gave worker delegates a formal voice in maintenance and safety planning. The Compact also made clear that manufacturing capacity could not be expanded instantly by decree.

### The Tidal Archive Crisis

The Nacrean archive dispute reached beyond Nacrean jurisdiction because the contested model informed regional route notices and treaty compliance claims. The regional Commission paused reliance on the disputed model, preserved independent observations, and convened a multi-polity review. This response did not erase political conflict, but it prevented one institution's disputed records from becoming an unquestioned regional verdict.

## 7. The Three-Current Crisis

The crisis begins when a new reconstruction of the Serein basin's seasonal circulation conflicts with the route model used by the Commission. The new reconstruction may be more accurate in some regions, but its data lineage is incomplete and its uncertainty estimates were not preserved in the first public release.

The consequences are uneven:
- Vey growers say the published route schedule discounts local observations and increases transport costs.
- Orison workshops face competing requests for components and cannot meet every accelerated repair demand.
- Nacrean delegates fear that regional actors will treat the archive dispute as proof that the Compact cannot meet its treaty obligations.
- Outer Drift crews argue that their incident records were used as raw data without adequate attribution or representation.
- Commission staff disagree about whether to suspend the model, annotate it, or continue using it with temporary restrictions.

No actor has a complete picture. Some claims are correct, some mistaken, and some strategically framed. The crisis is not a secret plot with one villain; it is a test of whether institutions can correct themselves under pressure.

### Escalation sequence

1. **Model release:** a revised current model is published without its full uncertainty and provenance record.
2. **Local challenge:** Vey navigators submit observations that contradict one regional prediction.
3. **Commercial pressure:** carriers demand a clear schedule; growers seek compensation for delays.
4. **Industrial bottleneck:** Orison cannot rapidly fabricate all requested replacement markers and relay components.
5. **Representation dispute:** Outer Drift observers demand access to the review process and recognition of their data contributions.
6. **Emergency proposal:** some Commission members seek a temporary corridor restriction; others argue the evidence does not justify it.
7. **Public hearing:** independent researchers, crews, workers, and affected settlements present competing records.
8. **Interim settlement:** the Commission publishes multiple route scenarios, preserves local observations, and adopts a time-limited safety schedule.
9. **Treaty review:** signatories negotiate representation, audit, compensation, and correction procedures.

### Possible outcomes

- **Repair and renewal:** the parties strengthen provenance rules, grant mobile communities a defined review role, and fund redundant observations.
- **Unequal compromise:** the main signatories restore predictable schedules but leave representation and compensation disputes unresolved.
- **Fragmented passage:** communities form parallel route agreements; access remains possible but becomes more complex and unequal.
- **Constitutional expansion:** the Commission gains a stronger public-interest mandate with independent oversight and strict limits.
- **Local autonomy settlement:** the Commission retains only shared standards and emergency coordination while local authorities control more route decisions.

A violent outcome is possible in a particular campaign, but it is not the default or the only dramatic resolution. The central tension is whether a regional system can distribute authority and correct error without making vulnerable communities pay for institutional pride.

## 8. Law, authority, and the use of force

The Serein treaties establish duties but do not eliminate jurisdictional conflict. A vessel may be registered under one authority, operated by a cooperative from another, carry residents with several legal statuses, and use a refuge maintained by a fourth.

For scenario design, distinguish:
- treaty signatories and observers;
- local jurisdiction and shared-service authority;
- rescue authority and ordinary policing;
- commercial disputes and political disputes;
- temporary safety restrictions and punitive exclusion;
- private security, public protective services, and armed force.

The Commission's inspectors are not a regional army. Local protective services have different mandates and oversight systems. If armed actors enter a scenario, define who authorized them, what jurisdiction applies, what rules constrain them, and how complaints are investigated.

The campaign's strongest dilemmas are often about restraint: whether to publish uncertain evidence, whether to delay a convoy, whether to compensate a community before fault is settled, and whether an emergency power should expire automatically.

## 9. Campaign roles and playable perspectives

Useful player roles include:
- a Nacrean treaty delegate balancing local legitimacy with regional obligations;
- a Vey navigator whose observations challenge the official model;
- an Orison fabrication coordinator allocating scarce repair capacity;
- an Outer Drift mediator seeking recognition without surrendering community autonomy;
- a Commission auditor reconstructing the model's provenance;
- a rescue coordinator responsible for evacuation and refuge capacity;
- a worker representative concerned about unsafe accelerated maintenance;
- a journalist or public archivist tracing how claims changed over time;
- a local council member whose community bears the cost of a regional decision;
- an independent researcher tasked with communicating uncertainty clearly.

Avoid assigning each role a single factional position. Give every character private obligations, incomplete information, and relationships across political boundaries.

## 10. Scenario frames

### The Missing Observation Window

A seasonal current measurement is absent from the public record. The team must determine whether it was never collected, collected but not transmitted, or excluded during model preparation. The challenge is to preserve evidence and trust while a convoy schedule approaches.

### The Refuge Capacity Vote

A refuge station needs expansion, but its location benefits some routes more than others. Players must compare population risk, construction time, ecological impact, worker safety, and representation. No option is cost-free.

### The Component Allocation Hearing

Orison has enough pressure-rated components for only part of the requested repairs. The players must establish transparent priorities, identify viable substitutes, and negotiate compensation without pretending that fabrication capacity is unlimited.

### The Observer's Seat

Outer Drift delegates request a formal role in treaty review. Signatories fear losing control of obligations they must fund. Players explore what meaningful participation requires: data access, voting rights, appeal rights, fiduciary duties, or a new category of treaty membership.

### The Expiring Emergency Order

A temporary restriction is due to expire. New evidence is mixed. Players must decide whether to renew, narrow, or end it, and define what review would justify a future restriction.

## 11. Abstract simulation model

These variables are design prompts, not a ready-made executable ruleset. Use ranges and uncertainty rather than false precision.

| Variable | Meaning | Typical influences |
|---|---|---|
| Passage reliability | Ability to complete planned movement safely | Current conditions, maintenance, staffing, route alternatives |
| Refuge coverage | Proportion of affected people who can reach viable shelter | Capacity, distance, accessibility, transfer time |
| Evidence confidence | Confidence in the provenance and quality of shared records | Independent observations, audit, uncertainty disclosure |
| Treaty legitimacy | Willingness to accept shared rules as fair | Representation, consistency, appeals, compensation |
| Repair capacity | Ability to restore service within a useful time | Components, trained labor, fabrication queues |
| Mobility inclusion | Whether mobile residents can participate in decisions affecting them | Legal status, registration, access to hearings |
| Ecological stress | Pressure on habitats and food systems | Harvest, construction, waste, seasonal variability |
| Escalation temperature | Risk that disputes move from negotiation to coercion | Threats, misinformation, emergency powers, trust |

Do not collapse these into a single stability score. High passage reliability can coexist with low legitimacy; high repair capacity can coexist with unsafe labor conditions. Model trade-offs and feedback loops explicitly.

A useful turn sequence is: observe conditions; publish uncertainty; identify affected groups; preserve essential service; negotiate temporary measures; review evidence; compensate losses; revise the treaty; evaluate whether emergency authority expires.

## 12. Setting continuity and future volumes

Future books can deepen this theatre without rewriting its foundation:
- a focused history of the Three Rescue Seasons;
- a Vey Shelf Assemblies political and social dossier;
- an Orison Works League industrial and labor history;
- a regional refuge-station and convoy campaign;
- a Nacrean constitutional sequel following the archive reforms;
- a LOD 4 scenario centered on one passage hearing or rescue mission.

New authors should record the date, institution, jurisdiction, and source perspective for every major event. If a later book changes a fact, identify it as a revision, local account, disputed claim, or alternate campaign branch rather than silently overwriting continuity.

## 13. Research and design notes

This is an invented setting and does not claim historical evidence for its polities. The research method is comparative rather than predictive: real-world treaty systems, maritime commons, shared infrastructure governance, migration law, archival provenance, and cooperative resource management can suggest questions, but no historical institution should be copied as a one-to-one template.

When adding real-world analogies:
- name the specific time and place;
- distinguish the source's account from the setting author's interpretation;
- include perspectives beyond central governments and commercial elites;
- avoid treating one society as a universal model;
- cite the actual scholarship or primary material used;
- keep the analogy's limits visible.

The central design principle is that a connected theatre is made by relationships and dependencies, not by placing several flags on a map. Trade, migration, law, ecology, maintenance, evidence, and memory create the conditions in which conflict and cooperation become meaningful.
