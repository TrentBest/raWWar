# raWWar — UX and Interaction Design

Status: Initial direction.

## Interaction philosophy
The player should interact with systems as a person inside the world.

The preferred interaction model is physical, contextual, and diegetic.

## Guidance
The world can provide subtle guidance without turning every action into an explicit tutorial.

A control that needs attention may visually or behaviorally invite interaction. A procedure can expose its next expected action through contextual state rather than a detached checklist.

## Information hierarchy
Information should be available at the level appropriate to the player's role.

A commander may need staffing numbers and readiness targets.

A soldier may need to know where they are assigned, what qualification is required, and what task they should perform next.

The underlying information can be related without presenting identical interfaces to every role.

## Menus
Abstract menus should be minimized where a physical interaction makes more sense.

Some management and configuration interfaces may still be necessary, but they should be justified by the role and context.

## Accessibility
Accessibility requirements remain to be defined and should not be treated as incompatible with diegetic immersion.

## Open interaction questions
- exact input model;
- desktop interaction conventions;
- future VR interaction conventions;
- diegetic versus abstract exceptions;
- accessibility modes;
- communication interfaces;
- spectator/observer presentation.
